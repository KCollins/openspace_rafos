# Explanation of Float data in Gulf of Mexico
These data were collected as part of oceanographic research projects in the Gulf of Mexico to measure the deep currents there.  The floats are deployed from ships, then they sink to a specific depth (in this case, either 300 meters or 1500 meters), and drift around for a year or more while being tracked with sound beacons anchored to the sea floor in several locations. At the end of their mission, they release a ballast weight, pop to the surface, and transmit the tracking data, plus temperature and pressure measurements, via satellite. We then use the tracking data to reconstruct the trajectories of the floats.  The path, or trajectory of each float is shown in a graph with the data files. The data files have column headings to define which variable is listed, and should be self-explanatory.

*The challenge:* to use data sonification to convey the path of the floats. For example, one could use sound pitch to convey latitude (north-south location) and panning to convey longitude (east-west location). That is a very simple sonification, and possibly not as effective as other sound designs.

*Extra credit:* use data sonification to convey the path of each float, while also conveying the changes in temperature, pressure, speed, and/or water depth along the float path.  

*Testing:* can a blind person, or a person with their eyes closed or blindfolded, draw the shape of the float path after hearing the sonification?
